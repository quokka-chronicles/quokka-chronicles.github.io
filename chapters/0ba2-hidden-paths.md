# The Hidden Paths

The next morning arrived soft and cool, the forest still wrapped in a thin veil of mist. The camp stirred slowly, creatures emerging from dens and hollows, stretching, yawning, shaking dew from their fur. {{qk.characterName}} woke with a flutter in her chest she couldn’t quite name. Every thought drifted back to yesterday — to Kevin’s quiet voice, his steady presence, the way her crimson line had glowed uncontrollably beneath his touch.

She tried to calm herself, but her heart refused to listen.

Today, the siblings trained separately. Warra went to sound‑tracking, Niran to speed‑runs, Koorli to water‑courses. {{qk.characterName}} was assigned to perception training — a lesson Kevin himself oversaw.

Her paws tingled with anticipation.

The training ground lay deeper in the forest, where the trees grew tall and close, their roots weaving into natural corridors. Kevin waited there, already speaking softly to a small group of students. His white fur caught the morning light like frost, and {{qk.characterName}} felt her breath hitch.

Kelvin stood nearby, cheerful as always, helping a squirrel adjust her stance. He waved at {{qk.characterName}} when she arrived, tail swaying in friendly greeting. She smiled back — she liked Kelvin, truly — but her crimson line remained still.

Kevin turned.

Her line flared instantly.

“Good morning,” he said, voice low and calm.

She nearly tripped over her own paws.

The lesson began with simple exercises: noticing subtle shifts in the forest run, recognising when a path curved strangely or stretched longer than it should, sensing when a clearing felt “too wide” or “too narrow”. Kevin explained that the forest sometimes played tricks — shortening routes, lengthening others, bending space in ways only the most perceptive could detect.

{{qk.characterName}} listened intently. She felt something inside her respond — a faint tug, a whisper, a pulse beneath her fur. When she walked, she sensed tiny changes in the ground, slight bends in the air, soft ripples in the undergrowth. She followed them, paws light, ears forward.

And she succeeded.

She slipped through a narrow gap between two roots and emerged several tail-lengths ahead of the group. Kevin’s eyes widened slightly. Kelvin laughed in delight.

“Impressive,” Kelvin said.

Kevin nodded once. “Again.”

They increased the difficulty. Kevin moved ahead, weaving through the trees with impossible grace. {{qk.characterName}} followed, trying to match his rhythm. She felt the forest shift around her — paths stretching, shrinking, twisting. She learned to read the signs: a leaf trembling too long, a shadow bending oddly, a root curving in a way that invited her forward.

She grew faster. Lighter. Quicker.

Kevin began to test her.

He darted behind a tree and vanished. She chased him, but Kelvin suddenly appeared instead, grinning, blocking her path. She spun, confused, and Kevin slipped past her from the opposite side. She lunged again — Kelvin replaced him. They swapped places effortlessly, moving like mirrored ghosts.

She never caught either of them.

Her frustration grew, but so did her determination. She pushed harder, senses sharp, breath steady. And then — she felt it. A tiny ripple in the ground. A faint pull to her left. A whisper of movement that didn’t match the forest’s rhythm.

She turned sharply.

A hidden path opened before her — impossibly short, almost like a single step stretched into a full run. She dashed through it, heart pounding, and burst out behind Kevin and Kelvin just as they swapped again.

She collided with both of them.

Not hard — just enough to tumble into their fur, her face brushing against their chests. Their scents overwhelmed her: Kevin’s cool, crisp, winter‑sharp; Kelvin’s warm, bright, sun‑soft. Her crimson line blazed so fiercely she felt heat radiate along her spine.

Kevin froze, startled. Kelvin laughed, delighted.

{{qk.characterName}} scrambled back, mortified, but her heart soared wildly. She had done it. She had surprised them both.

Kevin’s expression softened — just a little. “Well done.”

She nearly melted into the moss.

***

After the lesson, Kelvin suggested they visit Koorli’s training ground. “He’ll want to show off,” Kelvin said with a grin. “We can give him a proper surprise.”

{{qk.characterName}} agreed instantly. Kevin said nothing, but he followed.

They moved silently through the forest, slipping between roots and ferns until they reached a wide, slow-running curve of the forest run. Here, the ground dipped into a shallow basin filled with clear water. The otters, who were the lecturers for this part of the training, splashed noisily, laughing and teasing each other as they taught Koorli and his classmates. Their playful nature was clear — at one moment, they broke into an impromptu aquabel choreography, twirling and flipping in perfect sync, their sleek bodies cutting through the water like liquid ribbons. The students watched, amused and bewildered, as the otters' antics lightened the mood.

Then, without warning, the otters vanished beneath the surface, only to reappear suddenly, chasing the students in a lively game of hide and seek. Their quick movements and mischievous grins made it impossible for the students to predict where they would pop up next. The otters' teasing was gentle but relentless, a reminder that even in lessons, joy and laughter were essential parts of learning.

{{qk.characterName}}, Kevin, and Kelvin hid among the reeds.

Koorli practised diving into water so clear it looked like glass. When he leapt in, the surface rippled — but he vanished completely. No shadow, no shape, no movement. Only the ring of disturbed water and the shifting mud below showed where he had entered.

Even Kevin looked impressed.

Kelvin whispered, “Where is he?”

Suddenly, Koorli’s head popped up right beside them, on their right paws. They jumped. He disappeared again, and reappeared on their left. Then again — without moving at all — he surfaced in the exact same spot he had started.

The otters cheered. The students gasped. Koorli beamed with pride.

{{qk.characterName}} hugged him tightly, her heart full.

***

Kelvin invited them to eat afterwards. They shared roots, leaves, glowing fungi, and a few fish the otters had brought. Kelvin chatted easily, asking {{qk.characterName}} and Koorli if they wanted to join him and Kevin on the evening patrol.

Koorli hesitated. “Is it dangerous?”

Kelvin shook his head. “Just watching. Nothing more. If we see anything strange, we report it. No fighting.”

{{qk.characterName}} agreed immediately. Koorli nodded nervously.

They met at dusk, following the forest run against its flow. {{qk.characterName}} noticed how the spiders’ webs guided the forest — threads directing unwanted creatures away from the camp, bending paths gently so intruders never realised they were being turned aside.

The forest run narrowed until it became a thin, winding trail. Kelvin stopped. “This is our watch point.”

They practised disappearing into the forest, blending with roots and shadows. {{qk.characterName}} climbed a steep, muddy-rocky rise, curious about a strange pull she felt at its peak.

At the top, she saw it.

A hole. A tunnel. Dark, deep, wrong. The path leading into it stretched unnaturally far, like an endless loop.

Her fur prickled.

“{{qk.characterName}} — no!” Kelvin shouted from below.

She startled — and slipped.

The ground crumbled beneath her paws.

Kevin lunged.

He caught her by the scruff with his teeth — gentle, firm, steady — and her heart exploded in wild, dizzy heat.

But the rocks beneath them shifted, turning into loose sand. They slid together toward the hole.

Kevin made an impossible manoeuvre, twisting mid-slide with a fluid grace that seemed to defy the very laws of nature. Time seemed to slow, every muscle in his body coiling and uncoiling in perfect harmony. His eyes locked on {{qk.characterName}} as he propelled her toward the edge, the world around them blurring into streaks of green and brown. She landed hard, claws scraping against the rough earth, fingers trembling as she clung desperately to the crumbling ledge, heart pounding like a wild drum in her chest.

Kevin’s body arced in a final, breathtaking twist — a moment stretched into eternity — and then, as if swallowed by the shadows themselves, he vanished. The forest seemed to hold its breath, the silence pressing in, thick and suffocating. No sound, no movement, just the echo of her ragged breaths and the sharp scrape of her claws against stone.

Panic surged through her veins, cold and relentless. She reached out, but there was nothing — no sign, no trace, no hope. Kevin was gone. Lost to the darkness of the tunnel, swallowed whole by the unknown.

Helplessness crashed over her like a tidal wave, the weight of the empty space where he had been pressing down on her chest. Her crimson line flared wildly, a blazing beacon of fear and desperation, as the forest around her seemed to close in, vast and indifferent. She was alone, stranded on the edge of a void that threatened to consume her.

Time fractured, moments stretching and snapping back, leaving her disoriented and breathless. The world tilted, the ground shifting beneath her paws, and for a heartbeat, she felt utterly lost — caught between the fading light and the encroaching shadows, between hope and despair.

“Kevin!”

{{qk.characterName}} screamed — a raw, tearing sound that ripped through the forest. Pain surged through her chest, sharp and unbearable, as though the world itself had betrayed her. Her crimson line blazed violently, her vision blurred, her breath shattered.

And then everything went dark.

She collapsed into unconsciousness.
