# The Snow Brothers
Morning light spilled across the camp like pale gold, soft and cool after the night’s deep forest chill. {{qk.characterName}} woke early, her crimson back-line pulsing faintly with the gentle rhythm of her breath. Warra stretched beside her, ears flicking; Niran yawned loudly; Koorli rolled out of his moss bed in a sleepy tumble.

“Waterfall?” Niran suggested.

“Waterfall,” Koorli agreed instantly.

They padded out of the den and followed the familiar path to the lake. Mist curled above the water, drifting like silver threads. The waterfall roared softly, its spray catching the morning sun in tiny prisms. It was peaceful, quiet, perfect.

Until it wasn’t.

Two figures stood at the water’s edge.

At first, {{qk.characterName}} thought the light was playing tricks on her eyes. The shapes were too bright, too clean, too… unreal. Then one of them turned, and her breath simply left her.

Snow foxes.

Not the soft red of her brothers, nor the earthy browns of the camp’s scouts. These two were carved from winter itself. Their coats were pure white, shimmering faintly as though dusted with frost. Their fur caught the sunlight in a way that made them glow, almost haloed. They were tall, strong, elegant — every movement smooth and effortless.

And they were beautiful.

The first fox, slightly taller, stood with a quiet, steady presence. His posture was calm, almost solemn, his eyes deep and unreadable. His fur rippled with faint silver patterns, like moonlight caught in snow. When he breathed, the air around him seemed to shift, as though the world leaned closer to listen.

Kevin.

The second fox was similar in build but carried himself differently — lighter, brighter, with a spark of mischief in his eyes. His smile came easily, his tail swayed with relaxed confidence, and he radiated warmth like sunlight on fresh snow.

Kelvin.

{{qk.characterName}} froze. Completely. Utterly.

Her paws rooted to the ground. Her heart thudded so loudly she was sure the entire forest could hear it. Her crimson line flared in a sudden, wild glow, bright enough that Warra turned in surprise.

“{{qk.characterName}}?” he whispered.

She didn’t answer. She couldn’t.

Kevin had looked at her.

Just a glance — a simple, quiet glance — but it felt like the world had tilted. His eyes were pale, almost icy, yet warm in a way she couldn’t understand. Something inside her chest tightened, fluttered, then burst into a strange, dizzying warmth. She had never felt anything like it. Not fear. Not excitement. Something else entirely.

Kelvin noticed her too, but his gaze made her feel safe, comfortable, almost amused. Kevin’s gaze made her feel… undone.

The brothers, of course, reacted like nothing unusual was happening.

Niran trotted forward with a grin. “You’re new.”

Kelvin laughed. “We arrived last night. Long journey.”

Warra bowed his head politely. “Welcome.”

Koorli splashed into the lake without hesitation, sending ripples across the water. Kelvin joined him with a playful leap, both disappearing beneath the surface in a burst of spray.

Kevin remained still, watching the waterfall with a distant expression — until his eyes flicked back to {{qk.characterName}}.

Her line glowed again. Harder. Brighter.

She felt heat rise beneath her fur, a trembling she couldn’t control. She tried to look away, but her gaze kept snapping back to him, drawn by something she didn’t understand. She felt small, clumsy, ridiculous — and yet she couldn’t move.

Kevin’s ear twitched. A tiny smile touched the corner of his mouth.

She nearly fainted.

Warra nudged her gently. “Come on. Water.”

She stumbled forward, paws barely working, and dipped into the lake. The cold water helped — a little — but every time Kevin moved, even slightly, her heart jumped.

Kelvin splashed Niran. Koorli dove under. Warra floated lazily.
Kevin stepped beneath the waterfall, water cascading over his white fur like liquid silver.

{{qk.characterName}} forgot how to breathe.

***

Later that morning, the siblings separated for their lessons. Warra went to sound-training, Niran to speed drills, Koorli to water manoeuvres. {{qk.characterName}} chose a physical class — one focused on hiding, blending, and vanishing into any environment.

The instructor had not yet arrived, so the students gathered in a clearing ringed by thick ferns and twisted roots. The air was filled with a quiet buzz of anticipation and nervous energy. Some foxes crouched low, practicing stealthy movements, their eyes sharp and focused. Squirrels darted between tree trunks, testing the limits of their agility, while a badger clumsily attempted to bury himself under a pile of leaves, eliciting a few amused chuckles from the group.

A few students whispered excitedly about the upcoming lesson, sharing tips and tricks they had learned before. Among them, some were already practicing what they called "quokka crazy things" — playful, unpredictable maneuvers designed to confuse and evade any pursuer. These antics brought a lightness to the waiting time, a way to channel their eagerness and sharpen their skills even before the formal instruction began.

The art of hiding was more than just physical skill; it was a dance with the environment, a lesson in patience and awareness. Learning to disappear meant understanding the subtle shifts in light and shadow, the rustle of leaves, the scent carried on the breeze. It required tuning into the forest’s rhythm, becoming part of its whispering secrets. This was why the class was so vital — mastering hiding was not just about survival, but about harmony with the world around them, a way to move unseen and unheard, blending seamlessly into the tapestry of nature.

{{qk.characterName}} tried to focus. She really did.

Then a shadow moved at the edge of the clearing.

Kevin stepped out of the trees.

Her entire body locked. Her breath caught. Her crimson line burst into bright, shimmering light.

He was the instructor.

Of course he was.

The other students straightened instantly, tails high, ears alert. Respect rolled through the clearing like a wave. Kevin nodded once, acknowledging them, then spoke in his quiet, steady voice.

“We returned late last night. The Merged Lands are shifting again. New creatures. New… disappearances.”

A murmur rippled through the group, but Kevin offered no further detail. His eyes were distant, shadowed by something heavy.

Then he began the lesson.

He moved like mist. One moment visible, the next gone — slipping behind a root, melting into a shadow, blending into bark, vanishing into ferns. His white fur should have made him stand out, yet he disappeared more easily than any creature {{qk.characterName}} had ever seen.

He taught them how to breathe with the forest.
How to move with silence.
How to let the world swallow their presence.

He worked with each student individually, adjusting their stance, correcting their paw placement, guiding their breathing.

Then he reached {{qk.characterName}}.

She forgot how legs worked.

Kevin stepped close, his fur brushing lightly against hers as he adjusted her posture. Her crimson line flared so brightly she felt heat radiate along her spine. Kevin paused, glanced at the glow, and his lips curved into a small, teasing smile.

“Interesting,” he murmured.

She nearly melted into the moss.

He guided her gently, showing her how to sink into the ferns, how to let her breathing match the rhythm of the leaves. His voice was soft, patient, warm. Every word made her heart flutter.

Halfway through the lesson, Kelvin slipped into the clearing, and the entire group reacted at once. Tails lifted, ears perked, whispers fluttered through the ferns — the snow‑fox twins were legends, and everyone knew it. Kelvin offered a bright, easy greeting to {{qk.characterName}}, his tail swaying in friendly recognition, yet her crimson line stayed still. Not a single spark.

Kelvin noticed nothing unusual.
Kevin noticed everything.

And {{qk.characterName}} realised something terrifying and wonderful:

She was drawn to Kevin in a way she couldn’t explain.
And Kevin… noticed her.
