# Soft Reality

{{qk.characterName}} clung to the roots with every scrap of strength she had left. The earth beneath her paws was crumbling grain by grain, turning to loose sand that slid away no matter how tightly she held on. Sharp bark bit into her pads, cutting deep, sending hot pain up her legs.

Above her, voices crashed like waves.

“Hold on!” Warra shouted, his ears flat with terror.

“Grab my paw!” Niran cried, reaching so far he nearly toppled in after her.

“Don’t fall, please don’t fall!” Koorli sobbed, his whole body shaking.

Their faces blurred through her tears — three silhouettes leaning over the edge, desperate, terrified, calling her name again and again.

“Kelvin!” Warra yelled. “Kelvin, hurry!”

Kelvin appeared in the distance, sprinting across the forest run. His white fur flashed between the trees, his breath sharp, his eyes wide with fear. He was running as fast as he could — but somehow, impossibly, he wasn’t getting closer.

“Kevin!” Niran screamed. “Kevin, we need you!”

And then she saw him.

Kevin’s silhouette broke through the trees, running harder than she had ever seen him run. His body was a blur of white and silver, his stride perfect, powerful, desperate.

But he wasn’t getting closer either.

He ran like a creature in a nightmare — full speed, full strength, yet trapped in the same distant place.
Her heart twisted painfully.

He fell. I saw him fall. How can he be there?

Her grip slipped.
The roots tore at her paws.
Her strength drained away.

Pain surged through her legs — sharp, burning, unbearable.

And then a soft warmth touched her.

She blinked.

A quokka stood beside her.
Older. Gentle. Familiar in a way she couldn’t explain.

Her biological mother.

The stranger she had never known, yet recognised instantly.

The mother leaned down and licked {{qk.characterName}}’s wounded paws, soothing the pain with slow, tender strokes. The warmth of it broke something inside her.

“Mum…” {{qk.characterName}} whispered, voice trembling. “Mami…”

For a heartbeat, she felt safe.
Held.
Loved.

But the pain of Kevin’s fall slammed back into her chest, sharper than before, tearing through her like claws. The memory of her mother’s touch only made it worse — a reminder of everything she had lost, everything she feared losing again.

Her mother’s pouch shifted.

Four tiny quokkas tumbled out, squeaking softly.
They grew as they landed — swelling, stretching, becoming older, taller, until all four stood at her size.

All four were her.

One knelt beside her, examining her bleeding paws with worried eyes.

Another threw her arms around their mother, clinging tightly.

A third turned to {{qk.characterName}}, speaking with fierce urgency.
“We’re meant to be with Kevin. You know it. Stop holding on. Go after him. Now!”

The fourth stepped forward, calm and steady.
“We’re not ready. We need to learn more. We need the Sun‑Splitter sight. Only then can we find him… and save him.”

The ground shifted again. The roots tore at her paws even more.

***

The edges of reality began to fray, dissolving into a sickening, purple-tinged haze. {{qk.characterName}} clung desperately to life, her tiny paws digging into the abrasive bark of exposed roots and dry, brittle stems. The sharp edges of the stone bit deep into her flesh, a blinding, white-hot agony shooting up her arms as her strength rapidly began to fade.

Above her, the frantic shouts of her brothers pierced the roaring static of the void. They were screaming for her to hold on, their voices overlapping and fracturing in the warped air.

"Kelvin! Hurry, grab her!" Warra’s voice cried out, echoing across the impossible expanse.

From far down the slope, Kelvin came sprinting like lightning through the ferns, his white coat a brilliant streak of hope. But then, a cruel distortion tore through the scene. In the distance, framed against the bruised twilight, stood the unmistakable silhouette of Kevin.

{{qk.characterName}}’s heart seized in her chest. Kevin? But she had seen him swallowed by the dark.

Her brothers and Kelvin strained with all their might, heaving against the collapsing earth to pull her from the edge, yet their efforts were hopelessly frozen in place. And Kevin—though he ran with a desperate, life-or-death intensity, his legs churning furiously through the dust—did not advance a single inch. He remained trapped forever in the distance, running endlessly toward a rescue that would never reach her.

As her grip slipped further, a sudden, gentle warmth brushed against her bleeding, agonizingly pained paws. A rough, familiar tongue was licking her wounds.

{{qk.characterName}} blinked through her tears, and without a fraction of doubt, she knew the scent. Her biological mother. For one agonizing, beautiful heartbeat, she felt the pure, overwhelming surge of maternal love she had never known.

"Mami...!" she choked out, a helpless sob tearing from her throat.

Yet the fleeting comfort only magnified her torment, amplifying the crushing, unbearable pain of losing Kevin into a sharp, bottomless grief.

Then, out from behind her mother’s shadow, four identical figures materialized. They were other versions of {{qk.characterName}}—alternate selves, each one her exact age, yet bearing different scars and glowing back-lines.

The first alternate self stepped forward and wrapped her in a fierce, crushing embrace. The second leaned over to nuzzle and kiss her mother’s cheek with haunting tenderness. The third turned her gaze directly to {{qk.characterName}}, whispering into her mind with chilling calm, "The darkness wants you just as it took Kevin. This is your true fate... do not be afraid."

But the fourth alternate self shook her head, her eyes flashing with a sharp, crimson-slit intensity as she urged, "No. Learn everything you can about the long-haired heads. Only then will we have the power to find and save Kevin."

***

Glowing Caps began to push through the soil around {{qk.characterName}}, one by one, then in clusters — tiny, trembling bulbs, tall shimmering stalks, wide soft domes glowing in colours she had no names for. Their light pulsed gently, then faster, then all at once, as if they were breathing with her.

Her brothers’ voices cut through the glow.

“Hold on!” Warra shouted, his voice cracking.

“Grab my paw!” Niran cried, leaning dangerously over the edge.

“Please don’t fall!” Koorli sobbed, his whole body shaking.

Their shapes flickered between the rising fungi, bending and stretching like reflections in rippling water. She tried to pull herself up, claws digging into roots and stems, but the earth beneath her paws loosened grain by grain. Stones slipped. Sand trickled. Her grip burned.

“Kelvin! Kelvin, hurry!” Warra yelled.

Kelvin appeared far down the forest run, sprinting with all his strength — white fur flashing, breath sharp, eyes wide. But the distance between them stayed the same. He ran and ran and ran… yet never came closer.

“Kevin! Kevin, we need you!”

And then she saw him.

Kevin’s silhouette broke through the trees — tall, bright, beautiful — running harder than she had ever seen him run. His stride was perfect, powerful, desperate. His eyes locked on her.

But he wasn’t approaching.

He ran like a creature trapped behind glass — full speed, full force, yet frozen in place.
Her heart twisted painfully.

He fell. I saw him fall. How can he be there? How can he be anywhere?

Her grip slipped again.
The bark cut deeper.
Pain shot up her legs — sharp, burning, unbearable.

She wanted it to stop.

And then a soft warmth touched her.

Her fox mother appeared beside her, stepping through the glowing fungi as if they parted for her. She carried herbs in her mouth — crushed leaves, sweet roots, soft petals — and pressed them gently to {{qk.characterName}}’s wounded paws. The pain eased, just a little, just enough to breathe.

“Mami…” {{qk.characterName}} whispered, voice trembling. “Mami…”

The ache of losing Kevin surged back, sharper than before, tearing through her chest. The memory of her mother’s tenderness only made it worse — a reminder of every love she feared losing.

Kelvin reached for her.
His paw broke through the shimmering haze — warm, steady, real — the only real thing left in a world that was bending and stretching and cracking apart.

“{{qk.characterName}}… take my paw… please…”

His voice trembled, but the sound wavered strangely, as if carried through water.
The glowing caps pulsed harder, their colours bleeding into the air, into her fur, into her thoughts.
Her grip slipped again — bark tearing deeper, pain flaring bright.

Kelvin’s paw hovered just above hers.
Close.
So close.

But the world around her twisted — the edge of the hole bending like soft clay, the forest run stretching into impossible distance, Kevin’s silhouette flickering like a broken lantern.

Her heart lurched.

Two paths opened inside her chest — sharp, clear, terrifying.

One voice — her own voice, one of the versions — whispered from somewhere behind her ear:

“Let go… fall… he is there… in the dark… waiting…”

Another voice — calmer, steadier — rose from the glowing caps themselves:

“Hold on… stay… learn… become light… then you can reach him…”

Kelvin’s paw shook.

Her own paw trembled.

The glowing caps brightened — too bright — swallowing the edges of Kelvin’s shape, swallowing the forest, swallowing even the pain.

Everything narrowed to a single moment.

A single choice.

Her paw slid…
toward him…
or away…
