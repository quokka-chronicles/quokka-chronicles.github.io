# What Remains In Us
The last pale rays of the setting sun spilled across the clearing like liquid gold. The grass cooled under the rising evening dew, and the air carried a soft, honey‑sweet scent from the flowers splitting open for the night. By the stream, where crystal water murmured over smooth stones, {{qk.characterName}} sat quietly. The crimson line along her spine pulsed faintly with each quickened breath.

Kevin sat beside her. His white fur caught the final reddish glow of the day. He was close enough that she felt the warmth of his body, yet something about him radiated a strange chill — an invisible barrier he wore like armor.

She leaned her shoulder against him, heart thudding wildly.

“It’s beautiful here,” she whispered, afraid to break the fragile calm.

Kevin smiled — that small, reluctant smile he always tried to hide.

{{qk.characterName}} met his gaze. His amber pupil, a thin vertical line running from top to bottom, glowed like burning coals shifting through shades of orange. When it widened or narrowed, changing its angle, it was like watching a forest fire dance. His eyes held her captive, and she didn’t want to escape the heat pouring from them.

Time seemed to slow. The world shrank until only the two of them remained — two souls reaching for each other in a quiet blaze of tenderness and something far more dangerous.

“Kevin…” she began softly. “Sometimes, when we’re like this… it feels like the world around us changes.”

He stiffened.

“I know,” he admitted after a moment. “And it’s not always good.”

{{qk.characterName}} froze. The sun touched the horizon, splitting the clearing into a golden half and a half already swallowed by shadow.

“What do you mean?” she asked, her voice barely a breath.

Kevin lowered himself onto the soft grass. {{qk.characterName}} sat beside him — close enough to feel his warmth, but not quite touching.

“Sometimes,” he said slowly, “when I chase little mice… or when I smell blood… I feel like I’m becoming one of them.”

“One of who?” she whispered.

“The Mergers,” he said. “Like something inside me wants to step over a boundary. Like I’m… changing.”

{{qk.characterName}} inhaled sharply. Her heart cracked and flared at the same time.

“But you’re not like them,” she said quickly, too honestly. “You’re Kevin. You’re—”

“Maybe,” he cut in gently. “Maybe not. When I’m near you… everything intensifies. Everything speeds up. Everything… tears.”

She leaned closer.

“Maybe the world isn’t tearing,” she murmured. “Maybe it’s opening.”

Kevin looked at her, and the intensity in his eyes made her dizzy.

“{{qk.characterName}}…” he started, but the words died in his throat.

The sun slipped fully behind the horizon. The forest on the far side of the clearing drowned in an unnaturally thick, ink‑black shadow.

Reality shuddered.

The trees didn’t just sway — they overlapped, bent at impossible angles, arranging themselves into strange geometric patterns, as if a foreign forest were stitching itself into the world right before their eyes.

And then something stepped out of the rift.

A massive black creature emerged — a crosser the size of a monitor lizard. But its skin defied description. Was it glossy scales? Long feathers? Coarse fur? It was all of them and none of them. Its darkness swallowed light, yet somehow glowed with a twisted, black radiance.

A cloud of enormous black hornets poured from the tear around it. Their buzzing sounded like a distant waterfall — a deep, stomach‑turning rumble.

“Don’t move…” Kevin whispered, his voice trembling.

The creature and its hornets blended perfectly with the thickening night, yet their black glow gnawed at the edges of vision. One hornet broke formation, veering sharply. With a roar like a sudden gale, it shot straight toward them.

{{qk.characterName}} couldn’t hold still. Panic crushed her courage. She jerked just as the hornet landed on her shoulder. Its massive, glossy stinger plunged into her skin.

A hot, venomous bolt of pain tore through her body.

Kevin lunged, pinning her to the ground, covering her mouth with his paw. “Shhh—!”

But the pain was too overwhelming. A muffled, desperate squeal escaped her throat.

The huge black crosser at the edge of the clearing froze. Its empty, dark eyes turned toward them without a sound.

And the world collapsed inward.

Without warning, without any visible movement, the creature’s long, slick tongue shot forward. There was no time between the strike and the impact — in a single fraction of a second, the tongue wrapped around {{qk.characterName}}. But something was wrong. Was the creature still across the stream pulling her toward it, or had it appeared directly above her? Its massive shadow swallowed the clearing whole.

The tongue didn’t just squeeze from the outside. The wet, rough, black muscle coiled around her front legs, hind legs, tail, and head. She felt it latch onto her skin, trying to burrow beneath her fur, merge with her muscles, replace her body from within.

She couldn’t breathe. Darkness flooded her eyes, ears, throat.

“Kevin—!”

{{qk.characterName}} woke with a violent gasp.

Her chest heaved. Her fur was drenched in cold sweat, and the crimson line along her spine glowed with a dangerous, pulsing red light. She was in her safe, dry den. No crosser. No hornets.

Just a dream.

***

After the nightmare and the violent jolt back to consciousness, the world narrowed to the scent of dried pine needles, the honey‑warm aroma of healing herbs, and the dim glow of a fox den. {{qk.characterName}} lay on a soft bed of moss, her chest rising and falling in frantic waves, as if she were still fighting off the slick, black tendrils of that monstrous varan.

Her desperate cry sliced through the quiet like a blade. In the same instant, her three brothers shot upright from the neighboring moss beds.

“You’re alive!” Koorli blurted, and before she could react, he threw himself at her, hugging her so tightly she lost her breath.

“How do you feel? Pain? Where does it hurt?” Koorli and Warra fired questions at her, while Niran was already sprinting toward the entrance. “I’m getting a healer! I’ll be right back!”

“Niran—wait, stop!” {{qk.characterName}} rasped. Her head spun, the world around her felt fragile, layered wrong, as if reality had been stitched together in mismatched sheets. Koorli shoved a handful of her favorite juicy flower petals under her nose, but she gently pushed them aside.

She surrendered to the avalanche of brotherly hugs. Their familiar scent and warmth slowly anchored her back into the real world.

“I feel…” she whispered, swallowing hard, “…like some giant Canis tore me into a thousand tiny pieces. And now each piece is living its own life and thinking its own thoughts. What actually happened? Where are we?”

Koorli burst into speech like a spark shower. “You fell into the scar, you know? And we ran back, and Kelvin led us, and the spiders pulled you out, and—”

“Spiders?” she echoed, stunned.

“Yes!” Koorli nodded so hard his ears bounced. “They caught you in their time‑threads and hauled you out wrapped in tight cocoons! You slept three days and three nights before the healers even let us unwrap you! We stayed here the whole time. I stayed here the whole time,” he added with a glare at his brothers, “Warra kept running for food, and Niran kept pestering the healers to see if he could help… and he kept checking on Kevin!”

Her heart stopped—then exploded into a wild rhythm. The crimson line on her back glowed faintly.

“Kevin? Kevin’s here? Is he alright?”

“Don’t worry, he’s fine,” Niran said softly, squeezing her paw.

At that moment, the moss curtain at the side entrance lifted, and Kelvin stepped in — tired, but smiling. “Kevin’s awake,” he said. Before she could speak, a familiar quiet, gravelly voice sounded behind him.

“I’m alright, {{qk.characterName}},” Kevin said. His white fur was still ruffled, and his eyes held a strange mirrored sheen. “I just feel… like I have at least four different heads right now, and I’m not sure which one of them is talking to you.”

{{qk.characterName}} melted inside. All the fear from the dream, all the pain from falling into the darkness dissolved the moment Kevin lay down in the moss beside her. He was close enough that she could smell the faint ozone in his fur.

“Where were we?” she asked in a hushed, awed voice. “What happened to us in there?”

Her brothers immediately leaned in, eyes blazing with curiosity. “Yes! Tell us! What was it like in the scar? What did you see? Were there Mergers?”

{{qk.characterName}} stared into the empty air, her memories arranging themselves into strange, impossible shapes.

“I saw… my mother. My quokka mother — the one I never knew. And I saw Lira, our fox mother. I spoke with them. And I even… saw myself. Different versions of me, from different times. I talked to my own selves.”

Koorli inhaled so sharply it made a sound. “You saw our mother? Was she alright? We should’ve told her long ago that we’re alive, that we’re here…!”

“You don’t need to worry about that, young man,” came a deep, calm, authoritative voice from the entrance.

Alaric stepped into the den, his broad silhouette filling the space. His steady gaze settled on the small group.

“Our scouts delivered the message to your family long ago. We have a full report on the entire sector — your mother and her pack are completely safe. But now your sister and Kevin need rest. Their minds must recover from the pressure of the scar.”

He turned to the three brothers. “Koorli, Warra, Niran — Hugin and Freki are looking for you outside. Now that you know your sister is awake and well, you can help them prepare for the upcoming mission. Go.”

The brothers hugged {{qk.characterName}} one last time and slipped out into the daylight, relief shining in their eyes.

Alaric waited until the sound of their paws faded, then turned to the white fox and the small quokka. Kelvin made a move to leave, but Alaric stopped him.

“Kelvin, you stay,” Alaric said, his voice firm. “The three of you need to listen. That scar may not have been an accident. Things outside are changing — and fast.”

***

The forest was quiet.

Alaric walked ahead, his fox fur catching the striped light. Behind him followed Kevin and Kelvin, both still a little shaky after their recovery. Around them moved half a dozen spiders — large, furred, their eight eyes watching the world from eight different angles.

{{qk.characterName}} walked among them, still feeling faint echoes of the dream. Every step reminded her that only hours ago she had been wrapped in the black tongue of something that should not exist.

As they approached the stream near the edge of camp, Alaric spoke in a low voice. “Our scouts report more broken lands from nearly every direction. They’re appearing faster than ever. And worse… new types of Mergers. Some of these split‑beings seem able to open and close scars at will. We need to understand what happened here. How you, {{qk.characterName}}, managed to open this one. None of our patrols noticed it before.”

{{qk.characterName}}  swallowed. Kevin glanced at her — brief, but full of unspoken questions.

Before reaching the crater itself, they encountered a fox patrol. Räv and Elara emerged from the reflective needles, their rust‑colored coats shimmering. Greeting them quietly was a relief — the foxes’ eyes shone with pure joy at seeing Kevin and {{qk.characterName}} alive and awake.

“The area is calm for now,” Elara reported softly. “The nearest Mergers are at least one forest away.”

Alaric nodded and continued. But when they reached the rise overlooking the place where {{qk.characterName}}  and Kevin had fallen, he raised a paw and halted everyone. He turned to the spider engineers. “Do you see the scar?”

One of the massive spiders leaned forward, its many eyes flickering as it analyzed the space. Their eyes weren’t uniform black — each held a thin, glowing orange slit instead of a pupil. The slits contracted, widened, rotated, each at a different angle and speed, breaking the space into dozens of overlapping focal grids.

“The scar shows zero tendency toward spontaneous re‑dilation,” the spider intoned, its voice mechanical and resonant. “It is reinforced and stitched shut by a sub‑material time‑mesh. The geometry is stable. Internal pressure is null. Nothing from within will reopen it.”

Alaric turned to {{qk.characterName}}. “And what do you see?”

She focused her gaze. But instead of a yawning, impossible abyss, she saw only a muddy, rocky rise with a shallow dent at the top — a small crater. The air above it shimmered faintly, like heat over a spring, but nothing suggested the forest had once folded in on itself. If she didn’t know better, she would never guess this spot had been a shortcut through reality.

“Go a little closer,” Alaric said, signaling the spiders to stay ready. “Stay near her.”

Heart pounding, {{qk.characterName}} crept up the slope. Step by step… The shimmer intensified, but there was no sign of the devouring darkness. She reached out and scraped her paw through the dent. Only cold stones and wet mud.

She turned and waved. “It’s fine! It’s stable!”

Alaric, Kevin, and Kelvin climbed after her, the spiders following. But as the three foxes approached, something in the ground shifted.

{{qk.characterName}} gasped and grabbed a jutting branch. Right before her eyes, the space inside the crater stretched downward. The interior warped, lengthening into impossible depth.

“A‑ah!” she cried, a soft warning sound.

Alaric, Kevin, and Kelvin froze instantly — and the distortion stopped.

“Move back! Quickly!” she shouted.

As the foxes stepped back several paces, the space above the crater rippled and returned to normal. Just a muddy hole again.

{{qk.characterName}} exhaled shakily. “Okay… it’s fine now.”

Alaric narrowed his eyes. {{qk.characterName}} , come here.” When she reached the group, he issued a new order. “You two — {{qk.characterName}} and Kevin — stay here. Kelvin and I will check it ourselves.”

Alaric and Kelvin climbed the rise. Aside from the faint shimmer, they saw nothing. They walked across the crater, stomped the mud, tested the edges. The scar held firm.

They stepped aside but kept a clear view. The spiders positioned themselves around the crater.

“Kevin, {{qk.characterName}},” Alaric commanded. “Now you two. Together.”

{{qk.characterName}} looked at Kevin. He met her gaze. They took one step. Then another.

As they approached the crater, the scar opened wide — instantly, violently.

Reality tore like old cloth. When they reached the edge, the same bottomless darkness yawned beneath them, pulling at their bodies, tugging at their very existence.

“And now, {{qk.characterName}} … slowly step back,” Alaric whispered.

{{qk.characterName}} retreated one careful step at a time. With each pace away from Kevin and the crater, the darkness shrank. The scar tightened, folded, and with a soft pop, sealed shut.

All six spiders turned at once. Forty‑eight mirrored, ink‑black eyes fixed silently on the quokka and the white fox.

Their thin orange pupils — bright as crystal filaments — halted their rotating dance.

There was no fear in their gaze. No panic. No instinct.

Only the fascinated stillness of scientists who have just witnessed something extraordinary.
