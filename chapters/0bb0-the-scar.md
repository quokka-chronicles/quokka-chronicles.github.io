# Wounds in the World

The small grey paw slipped.

For a terrifying, endless heartbeat, Kelvin’s white claws scraped against empty air, catching nothing but cold ozone and falling shale. He saw her eyes—widened in terror, then softening with a sudden, devastating look of quiet acceptance and love—before {{qk.characterName}} fell backward, swallowed whole by the pitch-black void of the scar.

Behind him, Koorli let out a shriek so raw it tore through the freezing night air. The young quokka collapsed onto his knees at the edge of the pit, weeping uncontrollably, his small body shaking with desperate sobs. Kelvin caught him before he threw himself into the hole, wrapping both arms around the trembling joey. Koorli kicked, clawed, sobbed, but Kelvin held tight, whispering nonsense, anything, everything, just to keep him from breaking.

"Koorli! Up! On your feet!" Kelvin’s voice shattered the panic. His own chest was heaving, his amber eyes wide with horror, but a sharp, military discipline took over. He grabbed Koorli by the scruff, dragging the crying joey back from the crumbling edge. "We can’t reach them down there. Not like this. We need the camp."

Kelvin turned and bolted into the thicket, forcing Koorli to follow.

The journey back was a terrifying blur. Kelvin did not run along the ordinary forest tracks; he navigated the hidden, folded paths—stepping through narrow gaps between ancient kauri trunks where space pinched and bent. To Koorli, who was blinded by tears and breathless from sniffling, the woods seemed to warp around them. Trees rushed past at impossible angles, distances shrinking and stretching like pulled vine. He barely kept up with Kelvin’s swift white tail, his tiny paws burning as they scrambled over rocks that weren't there a second before.

When they burst into the slumbering sanctuary of the Red Fur Army, the midnight camp was deathly still. 

Koorli scrambled blindly toward their burrow, tumbling headfirst into the dirt inside. "Warra! Niran! Kaia— Kevin— hole— gone— gone— gone—" he wailed, his voice a frantic, breathless mess of hiccups and sobs. "She’s gone—the ground ate her—Kevin fell—{{qk.characterName}} dropped into the night!"

Warra and Niran leaped up from the fur pile, their ears perking in instant shock. Warra, the Listener, caught Koorli by the shoulders, trying to make sense of the panicked gibberish, but Koorli could only point wildly back toward the dark forest.

Meanwhile, Kelvin sprinted straight to the central alcove. He found Alaric resting beneath a slate overhang and barked a sharp, breathless command.

Alaric’s orange eyes snapped open, the thin vertical slits of his pupils widening instantly. He didn't waste time asking for details. Hearing the words a fresh scar and two fallen, the fox commander bounded out of his den and let out a long, low whistle that vibrated through the high canopy.

Moments later, Kelvin and Alaric stood beneath the ancient, moss-draped branches where the big web-weavers nested. High above, massive, shadow-dark arachnids shifted, their multiple crystalline eyes gleaming in the moonlight like polished obsidian."A scar has opened along the ridge," Alaric called up, his voice ringing with urgent authority. "Two of our own have slipped into the un-formed. We need your threads to stitch the tear and anchor them back."

A heavy, chitinous click echoed from the dark branches. The lead weaver, a towering creature whose outer shell rippled with a strange, liquid shimmer, leaned down. Its voice was not a sound, but a series of precise, rhythmic vibrations that resonated directly in their skulls.

“A scar can be sewn,” the weaver clicked, its tone calculated and cold as ice. “The shifting seam can be bound. But to fish a soul from the deep drift is a matter of tension. A single line cast into the un-space sways without rest. It glides past a solitary shadow. Unless there are twin points of weight to create a cross-knot, our threads will find nothing to pull.”

"They went down together!" Kelvin barked, stepped forward, his fur bristling. "There are two down there. They fell into the same pocket!"

“Then a cross-knot is possible,” the weaver responded. “Lead the way.”Warra, Niran, and a hysterical Koorli rushed out from the burrows, joining Alaric, Kelvin, and a dozen massive spiders that dropped from the trees on thick, shimmering draglines. Together, the strike team raced back through the night.

When they reached the ridge, the quokka brothers gasped in horror. To Warra, Niran, and Koorli, the spot looked like a bottomless, weeping wound in the earth—a jagged pit from which the cold, silent darkness of the night seemed to bleed out upward. To Kelvin and Alaric, it was a raw, trembling distortion where the forest's geometry had completely collapsed.

But the spiders saw something else entirely. Through their dozens of Sun-Splitter eyes, the web-weavers perceived the hidden, overlapping folds of the merged land—the extra angles and hidden depth where the world had ripped open."Begin the weave!" Alaric ordered.

The giant spiders lined the edge of the pit, their long legs moving with mechanical, terrifying precision. They fired thick, ultraviolet-glowing silk deep into the roaring void.At first, the glowing lines whipped wildly in the empty dark. The silk couldn't hold. It hummed and skittered off the shifting shadows like water on hot stone."It’s not locking!" Niran cried out, his paws twitching with frantic energy. "They’re slipping away!"

“Hold!” the lead weaver clicked softly. “The lines are searching. One point drifts... but wait. The second point pulls.

”Far down in the dark, the two falling shapes—{{qk.characterName}} and Kevin—resonated together. Their mutual presence created two distinct points of tension in the void. Suddenly, the spiders' silk caught the cross-angle! The glowing threads snapped rigid, vibrating with an intense, high-pitched pitch.

The web-weavers went to work, spinning furiously. They cast wave after wave of heavy, dense silk down the lines, braiding the shifting air and wrapping the twin signals into a massive, heavy mesh. The spiders pulled in unison, their powerful limbs working like living winches.With a sickening thwack, two heavy, solid masses were hauled out of the pit and slammed onto the mud.

The scar behind them zippering shut with a sharp, sycophantic hiss as the spiders sealed the remaining threads over the hole.The quokka brothers rushed forward, but stopped dead, horrified.

Lying on the ground were two large, thick, oval cocoons of dense, glowing silk. Inside the semi-translucent webbing, the shadowy shapes of {{qk.characterName}} and Kevin lay completely motionless. Neither moved. Neither breathed.

"No! {{qk.characterName}}!" Koorli screamed, reaching out with his claws to tear at the silk.

"Stop!" Alaric barked, swinging his fox tail to block the joey. "Do not cut the web!"

"She’s suffocating in there!" Warra shouted, his usual calm wisdom shattering into desperation.

"If you break that silk now, her body will un-anchor," Alaric explained sternly, his orange eyes dead serious. "The web is the only thing forcing their weight to stay in our world right now. Their forms are still settling. If you open those sacks before the weaver-craft cures, the scar’s pull will claim them again. They must remain wrapped until the healers say otherwise."

Silently, the RFA soldiers constructed litters from heavy branches. They hoisted the two motionless silk cocoons and began the solemn march back to the camp.

They brought the wrapped forms into the quiet depth of the healer’s burrow, laying them side by side on soft beds of fragrant moss and medicinal leaves. The silk around them pulsed with a faint, dying ultraviolet light as it slowly hardened into a protective shell.

Kelvin collapsed on the dirt floor right beside the two cocoons. He laid his head on his front paws, his amber eyes fixed on the white bundle holding his brother, refusing to move, refusing to sleep.

Warra, Niran, and Koorli huddled together on the moss directly against {{qk.characterName}}'s silk shell, pressing their warm bodies against the cold web. Exhausted, terrified, and drained of tears, the three brothers slowly drifted into a fitful, anxious sleep to the quiet, rhythmic hum of the healing den.
